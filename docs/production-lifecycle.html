Production House Lifecycle

How production houses actually hire, credit and run projects, and what that means for Ana's architecture and the production house experience. Built from desk research; to be validated with the first houses the sales team onboards.

1 · What real productions do

Six findings that shape the product

Each finding is followed by what it means for Ana. They come from public reporting and industry references, not interviews yet.

Hiring is layered, not central

The line producer or production manager hires heads of department. Heads of department bring their own teams: a DoP brings the camera and lighting crew they trust. Telugu producers describe each craft "bringing in several assistants".

So in AnaHeads of department must be able to build their own department inside a project. The house should not have to add every assistant itself.

Hollywood Reporter India; Wikipedia, Film crew

Most crew are freelance and move constantly

Crew work project to project across houses, on day or weekly rates. Union membership (FWICE, FEFSI, FEFKA) appears on CVs as a credential. The Telugu Film Chamber now lets producers hire non-union technicians.

So in AnaPeople are never "in" a company. They have engagements on projects. Union membership is a verifiable attribute on a profile, never a gate.

Deccan Chronicle; AIO Cine crew CV guide

Casting is often an outside company

Productions contract casting directors from the script stage. The flow is brief, search, auditions, tapes to the director. Firms like Mukesh Chhabra Casting Co. run their own databases and remote-audition apps.

So in AnaA second organisation can work on a project with limited scope: a casting company manages cast requirements and shortlists for another house's film.

Forbes India; The Print; The Week

Projects have several banners

A film is often "presented by" one house, "produced by" another, "in association with" a third, and commissioned by a streaming platform. Credits and control are shared.

So in AnaA project links to several organisations, each with a relationship and permissions. One of them is the lead that can issue credits.

Industry convention; to verify with sales

Projects live for years

Development can take years, pre-production 3 to 8 weeks, then shoot, post and release. Crew joins and leaves at different stages, and the team at wrap is not the team at the start.

So in AnaA project can exist with only a title and grow. Engagements carry dates and states. Nothing forces a house to complete a profile before it can act.

VFS; StudioBinder stages of production

No one owns the credit record

Final credits are compiled by the production from contracts. Public records such as IMDb are user-edited and unverified. There is no shared, confirmed record of who worked on what.

So in AnaCredits come from engagements the house confirms and the person accepts. This is Ana's trust asset and the main reason a house keeps its projects here.

2 · Rules

Five rules the architecture has to follow

The project is the centre

Hiring, requirements, conversations and credits all hang off a project, not off the company.

The person owns their profile

Engagements add to a profile. Ending one never removes anything a person earned.

Membership is about permission

Being a member of a house means you can act for it. It says nothing about employment, and one person can be a member of several houses.

Everything is progressive

A project, a department, a requirement can start as one line and fill in over weeks.

Both sides confirm a credit

The house issues, the person accepts or disputes. Nothing public until both agree.

3 · The lifecycle

Eleven stages, from sales handover to a verified credit list

Stages overlap in practice: a house hires for one department while publishing a requirement for another. The order below is the first time each happens.

Onboarded by sales

Owner, with Ana sales

Sales creates the house and sends the owner a claim link. No self-serve sign-up. Past films come in as projects so credits can be issued backwards.

Producer, owner

Add information over time

Synopsis, poster, schedule, locations, banners, platform.

Producer, coordinator

A short "what's missing" list instead of a completion score. Co-producing houses and a casting company can be linked.

04

Add cast and crew already chosen

Most early hiring is by reference: director, DoP, key HODs.

Producer, line producer

Invite a person to a position. They accept, and it shows on the project and, if they choose, on their profile as "working on". HODs get rights to add their own department.

05

Publish requirements

For the gaps: cast roles, crew positions, collaborations.

Casting, HODs, producer

A requirement belongs to a project and a position. Open to all, to invited people, or to past collaborators. Audition place and questions per requirement.

06

Discover people

Search for a specific gap, often for a person someone recommended.

Casting, HODs

Discover opened from a requirement is pre-filtered: craft, city, languages, dates free. "Invite to this requirement" instead of generic following.

07

Receive interest

Responses and interest arrive per requirement.

Whoever owns the requirement

A queue per requirement with counts on Home. Duplicate responses merged; late ones marked.

08

Evaluate

Compare reels and answers, shortlist, share with the director.

Casting, director, producer

Private shortlist with team notes. Share a shortlist link with the director inside the house. The applicant never sees a status.

09

Collaborate and confirm

Talk, audition outside the app, agree terms outside the app.

Casting, line producer

Conversations tied to the requirement and owned by the house. "Add to project" turns a candidate into an engagement.

Wrap and after

10

Establish credits

At wrap or release, the credit list is finalised.

Credits member, producer

A draft credit list generated from engagements. Edit titles, add missing people, send. Each person accepts or disputes. Published credits appear on profiles and the project page.

11

Manage the organisation

Ongoing: members, permissions, company page, all projects.

Owner

Members and roles, the company page, a projects list by stage, and an activity log for accountability.

4 · Data model

Eight entities. The engagement is the new one.

This replaces the audit's six blocks. The engagement links a person to a project, and it is what makes fluid careers, layered hiring and verified credits work together.

Person

name · disciplines[ ] · domains[ ] · city · languages · unions[ ] · availability

Belongs to no one. Disciplines can span Film and Fine arts.

Organisation

type: production house · casting company · gallery · publication
verified · profile

Onboarded by sales. Types added later without new screens.

Membership

person × organisation · permissions[ ]

Who can act for the organisation. Many per person.

Project

domain · type: feature · series · ad · short · exhibition
stage · dates · poster

The centre of work. Exhibitions fit the same shape.

Project link

project × organisation · relation: lead · co-producer · presenter · casting · platform

Several houses on one project, one lead.

Engagement

person × project · department · position · via (organisation)
state: invited → confirmed → wrapped · dates

One per job. A person can hold many at once, across houses and crafts.

Requirement

project · position · visibility · questions · audition · closes

Posted for a gap. Responses and interest attach here.

Credit

engagement · credited title
state: draft → sent → accepted · disputed → published

Issued by the lead organisation. Accepted by the person.

Why this handles every case you described

A freelance DoP on two films for two houses: two engagements. A casting director who works for three houses: three memberships, each giving her access to that house's cast requirements. A painter who also designs film sets: one person with disciplines in both domains, an exhibition project and a film project.

6 · Production house Home

A work queue across projects, not a feed

When someone switches to acting for a house, the same five tabs change jobs. Their personal Home stays personal.

  • Home: what needs a decision, ordered by urgency, then projects by stage.
  • Discover: find people, opened with a requirement's filters when started from one.
  • Create: project, requirement, or a post as the house.
  • Inbox: conversations grouped by project and requirement, owned by the house.
  • Profile: the company page, members and settings.

Each project opens a workspace with four views: Overview, Team, Requirements, Credits. This is where most of the eleven stages happen.

Tracking activity lives on each project and in an organisation log, not as a notification stream on Home.

7 · Validation

Model it on real houses next

Everything above is a hypothesis until three houses confirm it. Ask the sales team for one of each: a large banner with several projects a year, a mid-size house producing series for streaming, and an independent producer. One hour each, ideally with the line producer and a casting person present.

Questions to ask

  1. Walk us through your last project from green light to release. Who joined when?
  2. Who hires the camera, lighting and art teams: you, or the head of department?
  3. How do you find people today? References, unions, casting companies, WhatsApp groups?
  4. How many projects run at once, and how many people from the house touch each?
  5. When there are co-producers or a platform, who decides on cast, crew and credits?
  6. How is the final credit list built and checked today? Who signs it off?
  7. What would stop you from putting an unannounced project on Ana?
  8. What goes wrong most often between shortlisting and a confirmed hire?

What would change the model

  • If houses never let heads of department act in the app, stage 04 collapses into the house.
  • If confidentiality blocks early projects, projects need a hidden mode visible only to engaged people.
  • If credits are signed off by the platform, the platform becomes a credit approver.
